A robot that unloads a trailer has to look at a wall of cardboard and answer one question over and over: where does each box start and stop, and which one can I pull next without bringing the stack down? I compared every kind of method I could run end to end on a CPU: classical edge and plane pipelines on RGB, depth and lidar; two networks trained on synthetic walls; and three zero-shot "segment anything" models. All of them were scored on the same 200 simulated walls with exact ground truth.

Code: github.com/nadurthi/computervision · carton-wall-perception/ — the box-wall simulator, every method, SeamNet and YOLO training, evaluation, figures, trained weights and every result file (requirements.txt, commands in the README).

Main results

  • On simulated walls, a small network trained on simulated walls wins by a lot. YOLO11n-seg fine-tuned for 10 CPU epochs reaches a panoptic quality (PQ) of 0.92 (0.91 on the hard walls). A 1.1 M-parameter RGB-D U-Net that only predicts seams reaches 0.88 (0.87). The best classical pipeline reaches 0.67 (0.62).
  • On simulated walls, zero-shot foundation models trail far behind. FastSAM gets PQ 0.40 on its own and 0.45 with a depth-based selector. SAM 2.1-tiny with the same selector gets 0.63 on 30 walls. They split boxes into labels and tape, or merge whole stacks, and on this CPU they take 0.6 s (FastSAM) to about 38 s (SAM 2.1-tiny with a 192-point prompt grid) per frame.
  • Depth matters most when seams are faint. On hard walls with barely visible seams, RGB-only SeamNet drops to PQ 0.83 while RGB-D SeamNet holds at 0.87. On standard walls with clearly visible seams they differ by less than 0.01.
  • Lidar alone cannot separate flush boxes at this density. With 3 cm between rings, the best lidar-only method reaches F1 0.16. Projecting the camera network's labels onto the same points gives F1 0.92: segment in the image, measure in 3D.
  • On real photos the ranking flips. Both networks trained only on simulated walls break real boxes into pieces along print, labels and strapping. FastSAM and SAM 2.1, which never saw a simulated wall, give clean per-box masks on the same photos. Simulation was enough to rank methods and to train geometry, but not enough to train appearance.
  • Once a box is found, its pose is easy. Every method that finds a box fits its face to about 0.3° in normal and well under 1 cm (median) in face centre. Finding the right boundary is the whole problem.
