7 · Example 2: spin stabilization (reproduces exactly)

Detumbling a rigid body with principal inertias \(I=\operatorname{diag}(14,10,8)\) kg·m², using Euler's equations:

$$ I_1\dot\omega_1+(I_3-I_2)\omega_2\omega_3=u_1,\quad I_2\dot\omega_2+(I_1-I_3)\omega_3\omega_1=u_2,\quad I_3\dot\omega_3+(I_2-I_1)\omega_1\omega_2=u_3, $$

with \(l=q\|\boldsymbol\omega\|^2\), \(R=rI\), domain \([-1,1]^3\), the 59 CUT8 points, and the full degree-6 dictionary (84 monomials). There are two cases: high state cost (\(q=50,r=3,\mathbf u_0=-\boldsymbol\omega\)) and high control cost (\(q=5,r=30,\mathbf u_0=-5\boldsymbol\omega\)).

For both cases, policy iteration converges in 7–8 steps, and the ℓ1 selection keeps 3 of the 84 monomials:

$$ V(\boldsymbol\omega)=171.464\,\omega_1^2+122.474\,\omega_2^2+97.980\,\omega_3^2 , $$

the same polynomial the paper reports. It is exactly the analytic solution. Try \(V=\alpha\sum_iI_i\omega_i^2\), i.e. \(2\alpha\times\) kinetic energy. The gyroscopic terms cancel, \(\sum_i I_i\omega_i\dot\omega_i\big|_{\mathbf u=0}=0\), and \(\mathbf g^\top V_{\boldsymbol\omega}=2\alpha\boldsymbol\omega\), so the HJB reduces to

$$ q\|\boldsymbol\omega\|^2-\frac{\alpha^2}{r}\|\boldsymbol\omega\|^2=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\alpha=\sqrt{qr}=\sqrt{150}=12.2474, \qquad \mathbf u^*=-\frac{\alpha}{r}\,\boldsymbol\omega . $$

Both cases have \(qr=150\), so they share one value function and differ only in the gain \(\alpha/r\). This is the classical Debs–Athans result.

8.1 Why the rerun lands somewhere else

Two checks narrow it down.

The published V is not a solution of the described collocation problem. A converged policy-iteration solution satisfies its own GHJB exactly at every collocation point. Plugging the published coefficients into \(D(\mathbf u_{\text{paper}})\mathbf c=F(\mathbf u_{\text{paper}})\) at the 29 points described gives a residual RMS of 317, against a right-hand side of RMS 879. So the published run differed from the description in some respect, such as the point set, scaling or iteration count. The repository has saved workspaces only for earlier Gauss–Legendre runs, not for this one. Rounding of the printed coefficients can't explain it either: even worst-case rounding (±0.0005 on each, ±0.005 on the two-decimal \(x_1^3x_2\)) moves the residual by at most 28, under a tenth of it.

The described problem barely constrains V. The CUT set is symmetric under \(\mathbf x\to-\mathbf x\), and the Van der Pol vector field is odd. For an even \(V\), the collocation rows at \(\mathbf x_i\) and \(-\mathbf x_i\) are identical, and the row at the origin is zero. So the 29 equations have rank 28 over all 36 columns but only 14 over the 15 even monomials, and every iteration leaves exactly one even monomial to drop. The ℓ1 objective chooses which one, and the choice matters a lot:

Dropping \(x_1^3x_2\) or \(x_1^5x_2\) gives a controller within 21 % of optimal at the median, about as good as the published one. Dropping \(x_2^2\) or \(x_2^4\) gives one that diverges from some starts. The ℓ1 objective has no way to tell these apart, because it scores coefficient size, not closed-loop cost. On this problem the method's outcome is set by an essentially arbitrary tie-break. Adding a second, inner ring of CUT points (49 points) did not help either: at degree 10 and 12 the policies were worse still.

The general lesson. Policy iteration's monotone-improvement guarantee assumes each GHJB is solved exactly. With \(N\) collocation points and \(m>N\) unknowns, "exact at the points" leaves an \((m-\text{rank}\,D)\)-dimensional family of value functions, and the regularizer picks one. When the true \(V\) is sparse in the dictionary, as in the spin example, the ℓ1 pick is the right one. When it isn't, the pick can be far from the policy's real cost-to-go, and the next policy inherits that error. The quantity worth monitoring is not convergence of \(\mathbf c\) (the rerun's coefficients stop changing at the 10⁻⁹ level) but the gap between \(V(\mathbf x_0)\) and the simulated closed-loop cost.
