Sparse collocation for the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation

An optimal feedback law for a nonlinear system is the gradient of a value function \(V(\mathbf x)\) that solves the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman PDE over the whole state space. That PDE hits the curse of dimensionality twice: in the number of points needed to discretize it, and in the number of basis functions needed to represent \(V\). In 2015–16 we attacked both at once (Adurthi, Singla & Majji, JGCD 2017). CUT points keep the collocation grid small, an iterative ℓ1 program picks a sparse polynomial basis out of an over-complete dictionary, and policy iteration turns the nonlinear PDE into a sequence of linear ones. This page derives the method step by step and walks through the examples, with a Python port of the original MATLAB code.
